In mid-February 2022, the Oxford Hills Physics Department commissioned OHTS Pre-Engineering to design and manufacture a new laboratory slingshot apparatus. This was built to replace an aging wooden model that lacked structural rigidity and measurement accuracy. The project required creating an apparatus capable of precise force measurements and launch angle adjustments.

My team designed an aluminum frame anchored by two vertical towers. The larger forward tower holds the primary elastic cords, while the rear tower houses a hand-crank winch and tensioning cable. A central base pulley allows the slingshot elevation angle to adjust while guiding the tension cable inline with the frame.

After drafting material layouts to minimize scrap, team members cut the extruded aluminum frame elements while I collaborated with senior student Dawson Joseph to engineer the release mechanism. We developed a flip-latch trigger tied to the center cable using an L-shaped catching lever. A secondary hinged sear locked the lever until a release cord was pulled. All mechanical fasteners were thread-locked for safety.

Force measurement markers were calibrated along the main tensioning line to indicate potential energy in Newtons. The hand-crank winch permitted safe, controlled tensioning away from the release mechanism. The finished unit was assembled, calibrated, and delivered to the Physics Department in April 2022.
