In mid-February 2022, the Oxford Hills Physics Department commissioned OHTS Pre-Engineering to design and manufacture a new laboratory slingshot apparatus[cite: 5]. This was built to replace an aging wooden model that lacked structural rigidity and measurement accuracy[cite: 5]. The project required creating an apparatus capable of precise force measurements and launch angle adjustments[cite: 5].

My team designed an aluminum frame anchored by two vertical towers[cite: 5]. The larger forward tower holds the primary elastic cords, while the rear tower houses a hand-crank winch and tensioning cable[cite: 5]. A central base pulley allows the slingshot elevation angle to adjust while guiding the tension cable inline with the frame[cite: 5].

After drafting material layouts to minimize scrap, team members cut the extruded aluminum frame elements while I collaborated with senior student Dawson Joseph to engineer the release mechanism[cite: 5]. We developed a flip-latch trigger tied to the center cable using an L-shaped catching lever[cite: 5]. A secondary hinged sear locked the lever until a release cord was pulled[cite: 5]. All mechanical fasteners were thread-locked for safety[cite: 5].

Force measurement markers were calibrated along the main tensioning line to indicate potential energy in Newtons[cite: 5]. The hand-crank winch permitted safe, controlled tensioning away from the release mechanism[cite: 5]. The finished unit was assembled, calibrated, and delivered to the Physics Department in April 2022[cite: 5].
