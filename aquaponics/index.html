Developed over three academic years at Oxford Hills Technical School, this automated aquaponics system integrates aquaculture and hydroponics into a closed-loop ecosystem[cite: 5]. Originally started as a multi-team class assignment, the system expanded into a capstone build focused on continuous, automated water quality and nutrient regulation[cite: 5].

A Raspberry Pi operates as the primary logic system, sampling continuous sensor data to monitor water temperature, fluid levels, and pH balance[cite: 5]. Relays control power delivery to water filtration pumps, aeration units, and automated chemical dosing lines to maintain stable water chemistry[cite: 5].

Fluid delivery loops and grow bed layouts were designed in AutoCAD to optimize gravity drainage and prevent siphon lock-ups, while real-time operating parameters were piped to a local digital interface for active monitoring[cite: 5].
