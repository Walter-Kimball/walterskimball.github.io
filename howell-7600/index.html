During my internship at Howell Laboratories, the engineering team was updating the 7600 Sea Water Chlorinator because the previous model utilized outdated and unsourceable components. Because this machinery was assigned for direct deployment on upcoming U.S. Navy vessels, the update was designated a top priority. Near the end of the development phase, a comprehensive set of production engineering drawings was required for internal manufacturing reviews, engineering stamping, and official Navy approval.

Fellow intern Gavin Pardon and I were assigned to draft part and assembly drawings for the updated 7600 system with new part numbers and enter them into Howell's management database once validated. This process required strict compliance with ASME GD&T standards, Howell's drawing formatting conventions, and standard manufacturing notes. My focus centered on weldments, sheet metal bend developments, structural assemblies, and powder coating specifications. Each drawing underwent a multi-tier redline review starting with licensed staff engineers, followed by feedback from shop-floor manufacturing technicians, and culminating in formal engineering stamping and Navy sign-off.

Once approved, we entered the complete drawing set into Epicor ERP to establish active manufacturing jobs tied directly to CAD documentation. This involved creating new part profiles in Epicor and defining production routing operations—including raw material cutting, CNC machining, welding, and estimated labor times.

Our work saved Howell Laboratories close to a month on a 2-to-3 month schedule, reducing overall project turnaround by roughly 30%. The resulting efficiency prompted Howell to establish a permanent full-time drafting position. I returned to Howell in December 2024 to observe the Model 7600 enter manufacturing and undergo initial Navy compliance testing.
