During my Summer 2024 internship at Howell Laboratories, I was assigned to generate a complete 3D CAD assembly for the legacy 4870 Air Control Panel, which was still being manufactured using hand-drawn paper schematics. Because the engineering department planned to redesign the 4870 in upcoming quarters, creating an accurate 3D model was necessary for design reviews and client presentations. My task was to audit the Bill of Materials (BOM), model all constituent components, and produce a fully constrained CAD assembly and drawing set.

Sourcing off-the-shelf components revealed that several legacy parts were out of production and lacked digital models. To resolve this, I took manual measurements from physical warehouse inventory—including pneumatic fittings, pressure gauges, and air regulators—to create parametric models. In-house fabricated components were modeled directly from physical drawing archives and verified against physical stock.

I assembled the complete Model 4870 in Autodesk Inventor, configuring custom iproperties (part numbers, materials, and descriptions) to automate BOM generation within Autodesk Vault. I then produced the final assembly drawing package containing weld callouts, quality check dimensions, complete BOM tables, and production notes.

This modeling effort eliminated significant pre-project prep time for the planned product redesign and identified obsolete components early, allowing the team to contact suppliers for modern replacement options.
