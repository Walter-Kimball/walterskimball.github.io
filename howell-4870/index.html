During my Summer 2024 internship at Howell Laboratories, I was assigned to generate a complete 3D CAD assembly for the legacy 4870 Air Control Panel, which was still being manufactured using hand-drawn paper schematics[cite: 5]. Because the engineering department planned to redesign the 4870 in upcoming quarters, creating an accurate 3D model was necessary for design reviews and client presentations[cite: 5]. My task was to audit the Bill of Materials (BOM), model all constituent components, and produce a fully constrained CAD assembly and drawing set[cite: 5].

Sourcing off-the-shelf components revealed that several legacy parts were out of production and lacked digital models[cite: 5]. To resolve this, I took manual measurements from physical warehouse inventory—including pneumatic fittings, pressure gauges, and air regulators—to create parametric models[cite: 5]. In-house fabricated components were modeled directly from physical drawing archives and verified against physical stock[cite: 5].

I assembled the complete Model 4870 in Autodesk Inventor, configuring custom iproperties (part numbers, materials, and descriptions) to automate BOM generation within Autodesk Vault[cite: 5]. I then produced the final assembly drawing package containing weld callouts, quality check dimensions, complete BOM tables, and production notes[cite: 5].

This modeling effort eliminated significant pre-project prep time for the planned product redesign and identified obsolete components early, allowing the team to contact suppliers for modern replacement options[cite: 5].
