As part of the larger Aquaponics initiative, my teammate Caroline Sheets and I were tasked with developing an automated fish feeding system using the engineering design process[cite: 5]. The core goal was to build a low-maintenance, reliable system capable of feeding fish on a set daily schedule[cite: 5].

Taking design cues from commercial food machinery, we designed a rotating disk dispenser containing pocket cavities[cite: 5]. As the disk rotates, feed drops into internal pockets, slides across to an opening, and falls into the aquarium below[cite: 5]. This isolated dispensing path prevents rising moisture from reaching the primary hopper, keeping the stored feed dry[cite: 5].

I took over full ownership of the project during the second phase, incorporating transistors to replace heavy relays and converting a desktop ATX power supply to provide clean DC power rails for both motor driving and an Arduino Uno logic controller[cite: 5].

The final housing was 3D printed, mounting the DC drive motor above the food hopper with an enclosed drive shaft actuating the dispenser disk below[cite: 5]. The upper electronics enclosure includes an external LED status light to confirm system power, ensuring reliable daily execution[cite: 5].
